THERMAL DESKTOP VALIDATION MODEL
================================

Purpose
-------
Use this model to validate the solver's transient energy conservation and
solid-temperature response for the minimal rack case.

This is a sealed transient model, not a steady-state model. With a 1000 W heat
source and no fan, vent, or external heat sink, the temperature must continue
to rise. A steady solution is physically impossible.


Geometry
--------
Rack:
  Width:  0.5 m
  Depth:  0.5 m
  Height: 1.0 m
  Origin: (0.0, 0.0, 0.0) m

Aluminum block:
  Width:  0.5 m
  Depth:  0.5 m
  Height: 0.2 m
  Origin: (0.0, 0.0, 0.4) m
  Heat load: 1000 W total

Because the block spans the complete rack width and depth, it divides the rack
air into two separate volumes:

  Lower air volume: 0.5 x 0.5 x 0.4 m
  Upper air volume: 0.5 x 0.5 x 0.4 m


Thermal Desktop setup
---------------------
1. Create the aluminum block as a finite-difference or finite-element solid.

2. Assign the same aluminum properties used by the thermal-solver TOML:

     Density:         2700 kg/m^3
     Specific heat:    900 J/kg-K
     Conductivity:      205 W/m-K, or the exact TOML value

3. Apply 1000 W to the complete aluminum solid. This is a total heat load, not
   a volumetric value of 1000 W/m^3.

4. Set the initial temperature of the block and air to 20 degC, or exactly the
   same initial temperature used in the thermal-solver model.

5. Leave the rack exterior adiabatic for the basic energy-conservation test.
   Do not apply an ambient sink unless the identical boundary condition is
   added to the thermal-solver model.

6. For a conduction-only comparison, represent the upper and lower air spaces
   using air material volumes and connect their faces to the aluminum block.

7. For a natural-convection comparison, use FloCAD or a documented convection
   correlation. A lumped convection network will not reproduce a complete 3-D
   buoyant CFD field, so compare bulk temperatures and heat balances before
   comparing local temperatures.

8. Use approximately the same spatial resolution as the thermal-solver case.
   Perform a mesh-refinement comparison in both programs before comparing
   localized maximum temperatures.

9. Run a transient solution for 600 seconds with results saved every 10 seconds.


Analytical energy benchmark
---------------------------
Aluminum volume:

  V = 0.5 x 0.5 x 0.2 = 0.05 m^3

Aluminum mass:

  m = 0.05 x 2700 = 135 kg

Aluminum thermal capacitance:

  C = 135 x 900 = 121,500 J/K

For an adiabatic block with a uniform 1000 W load:

  dT/dt = 1000 / 121,500
        = 0.008230 K/s
        = 0.4938 K/min
        = 29.63 K/hour

After 600 seconds:

  delta T = 4.938 degC
  average block temperature = approximately 24.938 degC

The air adds a small amount of thermal capacitance. For 0.20 m^3 of air at
rho = 1.225 kg/m^3 and cp = 1005 J/kg-K:

  C_air = 0.20 x 1.225 x 1005 = 246.2 J/K

The combined idealized heating rate is then:

  dT/dt = 1000 / (121,500 + 246.2) = 0.008214 K/s

This gives an approximately uniform-energy temperature rise of 4.928 degC
after 600 seconds. Local solid and air temperatures will differ while heat is
being redistributed, but total stored energy must still balance.


Quantities to compare
---------------------
Compare these results between Thermal Desktop and the thermal solver:

  1. Integrated heat input: exactly 1000 W.
  2. Volume-weighted average aluminum temperature versus time.
  3. Total stored-energy increase versus time.
  4. Minimum and maximum aluminum temperatures.
  5. Aluminum center temperature.
  6. Temperatures at the upper and lower aluminum faces.
  7. Total energy residual.

For an adiabatic model, the primary conservation check is:

  E(t) - E(0) = 1000 x t joules

Start by validating total power, stored energy, and volume-averaged
temperature. Only compare cell-level temperature fields after those integral
quantities agree.


Optional steady-state validation
--------------------------------
To create a steady-state benchmark, add an explicit and identical heat sink to
both models. Suitable choices include:

  - Hold all rack exterior faces at 20 degC; or
  - Apply the same external convection coefficient and ambient temperature to
    the same rack faces.

Document the selected face areas, convection coefficient, ambient temperature,
contact conductance, and radiation assumptions. Without an external heat sink,
the 1000 W model must not converge to a fixed temperature.
