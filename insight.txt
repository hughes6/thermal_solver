Built and ran it — and it surfaced something much more important than the tau number itself. Full output:

Solid thermal mass:         7201.8 J/K

Two things here matter a lot more than the tau number:

1. Your fine mesh cells cap the stable dt at ~0.015s — lower than your current dt=0.05. That's computed straight from your own 
conduction-CFL formula (alpha·dt·(1/dx²+1/dy²+1/dz²) ≤ 0.5) using the actual dx=0.02 fine cells near the component. And it's not 
theoretical — when I ran your exact scenario for even 2 simulated seconds at dt=0.05, it triggered real warnings:

WARNING: advection unstable. CFL = 3.146 > 1.0
WARNING: condection unstable. CFL = 0.833 > 0.5
Your current run is likely already numerically unstable in the fine-mesh region — it may not be obviously blowing up yet (explicit 
schemes can limp along past a mild CFL violation for a while before it visibly diverges), but it's accumulating error the whole time.

2. This flips your dt plan on its head. Bumping dt to 0.5 or 1.0 wouldn't just be borderline — it'd be 30-60x over the stability 
limit, guaranteed to diverge outright. The direction that actually helps runtime here is the opposite of raising dt.

3. The real problem is bigger than dt alone. τ ≈ 17,000s means you need ~84,000-92,000s of simulated time, but stability caps dt
 at ~0.015s near the component — that's on the order of 5-6 million timesteps to reach steady state with pure explicit integration
  at this mesh resolution. That's not a "pick a better number" fix, it's a mesh/timestep tradeoff that needs a deliberate decision:

Coarsen fine_dx near the component (e.g. 0.02 → 0.04) — roughly quadruples the stable dt (CFL ∝ 1/dx²), at the cost of spatial resolution there.
Only run the flow solve periodically (you already have update_flow_interval for this — worth confirming it's set aggressively, since 
FlowSolver is also visibly maxing out its 2000-iteration pressure solve every call, which is real wall-clock cost per outer iteration independent of the thermal dt question).
Accept the long run and let it actually execute at a stable dt (~0.01-0.015s) for the full ~85,000s — this is the "just wait" option,
 but at your reported ~2 minutes per 200 steps, 5-6 million steps would be on the order of days, not minutes.
I'd start by tightening dt down to something with real margin below 0.015s (e.g. 0.008-0.01s) to actually fix the instability first, then 
separately decide whether fine_dx needs coarsening to make the total timestep count tractable — those are two different knobs and worth 
changing one at a time so you can see which one buys you the runtime you need.
