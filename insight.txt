Convergence — this needs separating into two different things, since your architecture actually has two different solves happening at different rates:

Flow convergence (pressure/velocity network) is fast and algebraic — it happens inside FlowSolver::solve()'s outer iteration loop, and it already tells you when it's done ("FlowSolver: nonlinear flow converged after N outer iterations"). Thermal convergence (solid/air temperatures reaching steady state) is the slow one — it's a genuine transient integrated by your explicit dt loop, and this is almost certainly not converged yet in your current main.cpp. Here's why: with sim_length = 10.0 and dt = 0.05, you're only simulating 200 timesteps = 10 seconds of physical time. Rough estimate of your actual thermal time constant, τ ≈ ρ·cp·V / (h·A) for server1:

V ≈ 0.222m × 0.222m × 0.044m ≈ 0.0022 m³, ρ=2700, cp=900 → thermal mass ≈ 5300 J/K
Surface area ≈ 0.14 m², and your solver's own printed h_est ≈ 11.5 W/m²K
τ ≈ 5300 / (11.5 × 0.14) ≈ ~3,300 seconds (roughly an hour) at that convection coefficient — and that's the natural-convection-ish end; with your fans actually driving air across it, h could be meaningfully higher and τ shorter, but this is a strong signal that 10 seconds isn't in the same order of magnitude as what's needed.

Your current fine_dx=0.02/coarse_dx=0.1 gives a couple of cells through the component's ~0.044m height, which is reasonable for capturing gross behavior; refine further only if you need more spatial resolution of a hot spot, not in pursuit of faster convergence — that's sim_length's job, not the mesh's.
