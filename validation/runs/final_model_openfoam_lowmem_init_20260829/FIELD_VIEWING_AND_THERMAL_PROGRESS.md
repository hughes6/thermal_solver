# Viewing OpenFOAM fluid fields, hotspots, and recirculation

This guide applies to the final research-lab case generated by Thermal Sim v2.3.
Two cases are intentionally retained from the 2026-08-29 campaign:

```text
Strict airflow case (preserved):
C:\OpenFOAM\thermal_model_final\final_model_openfoam_lowmem_init_20260829

Unvalidated thermal-screening fork:
C:\OpenFOAM\thermal_model_final\final_model_openfoam_lowmem_init_20260829_screening_thermal_from_0p35
```

Each case has one `fluid` region and sixteen conjugate solid regions. Always
check both the case path and time shown in the viewer. A reconstructed
checkpoint appears at the case root; processor-only fields under `processor0`
and `processor1` are restart data and must not be opened as if one processor
contained the complete rack.

## Current campaign status (2026-08-29)

The strict case reached 0.35 s and **did not pass** its airflow gate. It remains
unchanged with `.initial_airflow_pending` present and no
`.initial_airflow_converged` marker. Its exact audit was:

- exterior mass imbalance: 0.221771%, passing the 1% gate;
- all 14 ambient fan directions and all 30 internal fan directions: passing;
- cumulative rack-air exchange fraction: 0.0990305;
- latest and previous spatial velocity RMS changes: 3.51968% and 3.54559%,
  both above the 3% gate;
- fan positive-pressure domain: 11 failures and 2 warnings; and
- worst fan-domain utilization: 348.475%, at Thruster exhaust fan 2.

The fan-domain failures are physical-model failures, not a CFM/unit or fan
multiplicity bug. Rack suction is pulling air through fan openings beyond the
first zero-pressure point of their supplied curves. The exported curve tables
clamp pressure to zero there and contain no negative-pressure/windmilling
resistance. The Thruster source data is also internally inconsistent: it calls
for 18 CFM while the supplied Sunon polynomial first reaches zero at
13.764 CFM.

To obtain early thermal behavior without falsifying that result, a separate
fork froze the exact 0.35 s velocity field, restored all heat sources, and ran
15 implicit thermal-only steps to 300 s. It used two MPI ranks, two outer
energy correctors, and a 19.9767 s timestep. The thermal stage completed in
140.429 s wall time, reconstructed every region at 300 s, and produced no
fatal error, NaN, OOM, or interrupted field. The final energy solves reached
residuals below 1e-6.

This is a **screening checkpoint, not a converged or validated solution**. The
thermal convergence baseline was only created at 300 s, its convergence streak
is zero, and terminal live-airflow validation is still pending. The fork's
`.initial_airflow_converged` marker was deliberately synthesized by the
documented screening override; it is not evidence that the strict audit passed.
See `SCREENING_THERMAL_OVERRIDE.md` in the fork before using its results.

## 1. Create a ParaView entry file

An OpenFOAM `.foam` file is an empty marker, not a copy of the solution. From
PowerShell:

```powershell
$StrictCase = 'C:\OpenFOAM\thermal_model_final\final_model_openfoam_lowmem_init_20260829'
$ThermalCase = 'C:\OpenFOAM\thermal_model_final\final_model_openfoam_lowmem_init_20260829_screening_thermal_from_0p35'
New-Item -ItemType File -Path (Join-Path $StrictCase 'final_model.foam') -Force
New-Item -ItemType File -Path (Join-Path $ThermalCase 'final_model.foam') -Force
```

Open the strict marker for the 0.35 s airflow field or the screening marker for
the 300 s temperature field. If `paraview.exe` is on `PATH`:

```powershell
paraview.exe (Join-Path $ThermalCase 'final_model.foam')
```

In the OpenFOAM reader properties:

1. Select reconstructed time `0.35` in the strict case for the airflow audit,
   or `300` in the screening fork for the thermal snapshot, then click
   **Apply**.
2. Enable the `fluid` mesh region for airflow. Enable the solid regions for
   component temperatures.
3. Enable at least `U`, `T`, `p`, `p_rgh`, `k`, `omega`, and `Co` in **Cell
   Arrays**. Click **Apply** again.
4. Use **Rescale to Data Range** after changing time or field. A fixed color
   range is better when comparing two checkpoints.

Do not use the screening fork's synthetic convergence marker as proof of
airflow acceptance. Check `.screening_airflow_override` and
`SCREENING_THERMAL_OVERRIDE.md` first. `run_summary.log` records the strict
audit failure, the override, and every subsequent thermal stage.

## 2. Fluid velocity, pressure, and temperature fields

For a rack cut plane in ParaView:

1. Select the OpenFOAM case in the Pipeline Browser.
2. Use **Filters > Alphabetical > Extract Block** and retain `fluid`.
3. Add **Slice**. The project coordinates are `x` left-to-right, `y`
   front-to-rear, and `z` bottom-to-top.
4. Color by `U` and choose **Magnitude** for speed, or color by `T`, `p_rgh`,
   `k`, or `omega`.
5. Add **Glyph** to the slice, set **Vectors** to `U`, and reduce the glyph
   density with masking or sampling if the display is crowded.

Useful cuts are a center-depth `y` slice for rack-wide vertical structure, an
`x` slice through a component centerline, and a `z` slice through the fan rows.
Pressure should normally be inspected with `p_rgh` for buoyant-flow structure;
`p` includes the hydrostatic contribution.

The bundled PyVista viewer provides a repeatable command-line equivalent. Run
these commands from the v2.3 project directory:

```powershell
$ThermalCase = 'C:\OpenFOAM\thermal_model_final\final_model_openfoam_lowmem_init_20260829_screening_thermal_from_0p35'
python .\plot\fluid_results.py --case $ThermalCase --time 300 --field speed `
  --slice-axis y --vectors --save --output fluid_speed_300.png
python .\plot\fluid_results.py --case $ThermalCase --time 300 --field temperature `
  --slice-axis y --contours 20 --save --output fluid_temperature_300.png
python .\plot\fluid_results.py --case $ThermalCase --time 300 --field p_rgh `
  --slice-axis y --save --output fluid_pressure_300.png
```

Omit `--save` for an interactive window. Use an explicit numerical `--time`
when producing evidence; `latest` can silently change after a continuation.
The 300 s velocity and pressure fields are the frozen 0.35 s screening seed,
not a thermally refreshed flow solution.

## 3. Hotspots and component temperatures

The temperature viewer reads the fluid and all solid regions, reports the
global maximum and its coordinate, and marks it in the render:

```powershell
$ThermalCase = 'C:\OpenFOAM\thermal_model_final\final_model_openfoam_lowmem_init_20260829_screening_thermal_from_0p35'
python .\plot\heat_animation.py --format openfoam --case $ThermalCase --time 300 `
  --slice-axis none --temperature-units C --save --output hotspot_300.png
python .\plot\heat_animation.py --format openfoam --case $ThermalCase `
  --convergence-report --temperature-units C --output temperature_history.png
```

The convergence report also writes a CSV beside the PNG. For evidence, retain
both files and record the checkpoint time.

For exact, low-memory tabular evidence without PyVista, run from the v2.3
project directory:

```powershell
$ThermalCase = 'C:\OpenFOAM\thermal_model_final\final_model_openfoam_lowmem_init_20260829_screening_thermal_from_0p35'
$Out = '.\validation\runs\final_model_openfoam_lowmem_init_20260829\thermal_screening_300'
New-Item -ItemType Directory -Path $Out -Force | Out-Null
python .\tools\openfoam_component_report.py $ThermalCase --time 300 `
  --csv (Join-Path $Out 'component_temperatures_t300.csv') `
  --json (Join-Path $Out 'component_temperatures_t300.json') `
  --markdown (Join-Path $Out 'component_temperatures_t300.md')
```

Use numeric `--time 300`; this tool does not accept the literal `latest`.

In ParaView, enable all solid blocks, color by `T`, and use **Find Data** or the
Spreadsheet View to locate the maximum cell value. A surface render is useful
for locating the component; a slice through that coordinate is required to see
whether the peak is internal. Report both the maximum and a volume-average
temperature. A single-cell maximum alone can be mesh-sensitive.

Temperatures from the sub-second strict audit are initialization values, not
thermal conclusions. The 300 s fork restored the complete 2434.8 W heat-source
set, but its temperatures remain unvalidated because the airflow seed failed
the strict gate and no live buoyancy/temperature feedback occurred during the
thermal-only interval.

## 4. Streamlines and spatial recirculation zones

In ParaView, select the extracted `fluid` block and add **Stream Tracer**:

1. Use a **Point Source**, **High Resolution Line Source**, or **Plane Source**
   just downstream of an exhaust, across the rear plenum, or immediately in
   front of the rack intakes.
2. Set integration direction to **Both** and color the streamlines by `U`
   magnitude or `T`.
3. Add **Tube** for visibility. Repeat with seeds at several vents because one
   seed plane can miss a localized return path.
4. A streamline leaving an exhaust region and returning to an intake indicates
   spatial recirculation. Low-speed pockets alone are stagnation zones, not
   proof of exhaust-to-intake recirculation.

Repeatable bundled commands are:

```powershell
$ThermalCase = 'C:\OpenFOAM\thermal_model_final\final_model_openfoam_lowmem_init_20260829_screening_thermal_from_0p35'
python .\plot\fluid_results.py --case $ThermalCase --time 300 --field speed `
  --slice-axis none --streamlines --seed vents --streamline-direction both `
  --streamline-color speed --seed-count 200 --save `
  --output vent_streamlines_300.png
python .\plot\fluid_results.py --case $ThermalCase --time 300 --field temperature `
  --slice-axis none --streamlines --seed fans --streamline-direction both `
  --streamline-color temperature --seed-count 200 --save `
  --output fan_streamlines_temperature_300.png
```

The rack center coordinates are approximately `x=0.29115 m`, `y=0.54674 m`,
and `z=0.889 m`. An `x`-normal slice at the center is a useful side view; a
`z`-normal center slice is a useful plan view.

For vortex diagnostics in ParaView, apply **Gradient** to the fluid volume with
`U` as the input vector, enable **Vorticity** and **Q Criterion**, then contour
positive Q. Q-criterion identifies rotation-dominated structures; it does not
by itself prove that hot exhaust reaches an intake. Confirm suspected zones
with temperature-colored streamlines and intake temperature histories.

For a dominant front-to-rear flow, a **Calculator** result such as `-U_Y` can
help reveal locally reversed `y` velocity, followed by **Threshold** above
zero. This is only valid where the expected local direction is known; many
components in this model intentionally use other axes.

The present model terminates at prescribed ambient intake and exhaust patches;
it does not include the room volume outside those patches. Consequently, it
can show internal return flow, eddies, stagnation, and cross-coupling inside
the rack, but it cannot resolve an exhaust plume traveling through the room
and re-entering a rack inlet. Add an external-room domain or validated intake
boundary temperatures for that question.

## 5. Quantitative hot-air recirculation report

The recirculation report complements streamlines. It uses signed boundary mass
flow and mass-weighted temperature to calculate intake warming, heat rejection,
and a recirculation-temperature index, and writes plot and CSV evidence. The
following commands use only the Python standard library on this host:

```powershell
$StrictCase = 'C:\OpenFOAM\thermal_model_final\final_model_openfoam_lowmem_init_20260829'
$ThermalCase = 'C:\OpenFOAM\thermal_model_final\final_model_openfoam_lowmem_init_20260829_screening_thermal_from_0p35'
python .\plot\recirculation_report.py --case $StrictCase `
  --snapshot-times 0.35 --csv-only `
  --output .\validation\runs\final_model_openfoam_lowmem_init_20260829\airflow_audit_0p35\airflow_recirculation_0p35.csv
python .\plot\recirculation_report.py --case $ThermalCase `
  --snapshot-times 300 --csv-only `
  --output .\validation\runs\final_model_openfoam_lowmem_init_20260829\thermal_screening_300\recirculation_t300.csv
```

The CSV-only run also creates per-opening face-flow, internal-air, and
internal-velocity CSVs beside the main output. In this case all modeled ambient
inflows are prescribed at 293.15 K, so a zero thermal re-ingestion index is
expected and does **not** prove that an unmodeled room plume cannot recirculate.
A hot intake index would show warming but would not identify the source.
Spatial attribution requires streamlines or a transported exhaust tracer. The
project includes `openfoam_tracer_solver/steadyExhaustTracerFoam` for a later
source-attribution campaign; do not infer source attribution from temperature
alone.

At 300 s the frozen field gives 0.661322 kg/s intake and 0.662791 kg/s exhaust,
with 0.221614% boundary imbalance. Exhaust air is 294.412 K versus the
prescribed 293.150 K intake, corresponding to 840.926 W sensible heat rejection
or 34.538% of the applied 2434.8 W at this early checkpoint. Exterior thermal
re-ingestion and bidirectional-patch fractions are both zero.

The internal-velocity report flags six **candidate** reverse-flow vent zones at
300 s. These are configured-zone, expected-axis averages, not proof of a closed
recirculation loop:

| Component / zone | Expected-axis velocity | Priority for streamline seeding |
|---|---:|---|
| NI chassis, top-right side vent | -0.7110 m/s | High |
| Cisco switch, side-left intake | -0.3956 m/s | High |
| NI chassis, top-left side vent | -0.0598 m/s | Medium |
| Keysight N6701C, left-front intake | -0.0499 m/s | Medium |
| Keysight N6701C, right-front intake | -0.0116 m/s | Low |
| Keysight N5766A, right-front intake | -0.00032 m/s | Near-zero; may be numerical |

Seed streamlines on both sides of these six zones, color by temperature, and
confirm whether paths return to the same or another opening. The strict fan
direction audit still passed; these vent-zone flags are not fan-direction

## 6. Airflow-audit and thermal-stage interpretation

For this generated case, cold-start airflow acceptance is intentionally
fail-closed. It requires all of the following:

- at least 0.30 s of live flow after the 0.05 s fan ramp;
- at least 1% cumulative rack-air exchange;
- exterior mass imbalance no greater than 1%;
- maximum tracked device-flow change no greater than 1%;
- the latest two spatial velocity RMS changes no greater than 3%;
- correct fan directions; and
- every fan inside its positive-pressure curve domain.

The low-memory profile currently sets the exchange fraction to `0.01`. The
nearby profile comment calls this "one rack air replacement," but the runner's
dimensionless integration makes `1.0` one full replacement. The implemented
gate therefore requires 1%, not one full replacement. This discrepancy is
recorded here and is another reason to classify the present run as screening.

The live-flow safety window is limited to 0.50 s beyond the observation start.
On acceptance, the runner writes `.initial_airflow_converged`, preserves the
accepted velocity reference, restores full fluid heat sources, and switches to
implicit thermal-only steps. Thermal-only operation holds the accepted airflow
and uses a 20 s maximum timestep, with periodic live-flow refreshes. It is much
faster than advancing hundreds of seconds with the live CFD timestep.

The existence of a 300 s field is not a steady-state claim. The production
thermal convergence gate cannot pass before 2400 s and additionally requires
component peak changes no greater than 0.25 K per 300 s, component-average
changes no greater than 0.10 K per 300 s, and validated airflow refreshes.

## 7. What the 300 s result does and does not establish

The screening run demonstrates that the low-memory multirate path can advance
the full 16-solid, 2434.8 W CHT model hundreds of physical seconds in minutes
once airflow is frozen. It also identifies where to investigate first:

- Trenton and NI have very large peak-to-average temperature splits, so inspect
  their source-zone volumes, pseudo-electronic conductivity, coupled-interface
  cells, and local mesh before accepting their peaks as hardware temperatures.
- Keysight N6701C has the highest component volume average at 38.93 C and is a
  more broadly heated body than the very localized Trenton/NI maxima.
- Only 34.54% of applied power is leaving as sensible exhaust heat at 300 s;
  substantial energy is still accumulating in the modeled thermal mass. This
  is consistent with a transient checkpoint, not thermal equilibrium.
- The final 19.9767 s step still increased the Trenton peak by 4.554 K, NI by
  2.168 K, Meanwell unit 1 by 1.634 K, and the Keysight N6701C volume average
  by 1.041 K. These rates are far above the formal convergence thresholds.
- All 17 reported wall/interface patches span mixed or buffer-layer y+ values.
  Rack-wall y+ ranges from 1.46 to 417.47, and the maximum interface value is
  545.58 at the DIN-rail region. These frozen-flow y+ values are not evidence
  of mesh-independent wall heat transfer.
- Full `checkMesh` reports reduced-order determinant warnings in 14 regions.
  Other full geometry/topology checks pass, but the warnings remain a mesh
  qualification item.
- The inherited 0.35 s aerodynamic state still has 11 fan-curve-domain
  failures. There is no new 300 s fan operating point because `U`, turbulence,
  and momentum were held during the thermal-only stage.
- The maximum printed Courant number near 70,140 is diagnostic in this mode,
  not a live-CFD stability measure. The energy equations were implicit while
  momentum and transport fields were frozen; a live-flow solve cannot use the
  20 s thermal timestep.

Accordingly, the run is useful for solver throughput, field-viewing workflow,
hotspot triage, and early thermal trend screening. It does not validate fan
operating points, external room recirculation, grid independence, steady-state
temperatures, or industry readiness.

## 8. Evidence checklist

For any screenshot or report used in a decision, record:

- exact case path and reconstructed checkpoint time;
- model/fan/config provenance from the case `provenance` directory;
- field name, units, slice coordinate, color limits, and streamline seed;
- whether `.initial_airflow_converged` was produced by the strict audit or by
  the documented screening override;
- `run_summary.log` airflow/thermal acceptance entries;
- Courant, continuity, boundary-balance, and fan-domain status; and
- the known geometry and material assumptions in
  `validation/UPDATED_MODEL_ASSUMPTIONS.toml`.

This campaign remains engineering screening. The incomplete NI separator,
approximate rail/obstruction geometry, provisional fan curves and loads, and
homogenized materials prevent an as-built or industry-release validation claim.
